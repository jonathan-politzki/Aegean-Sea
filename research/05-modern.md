# Fishing in the Aegean, 1821–2026: a sourced dossier

*Research file 05 — "modern" period (Greek War of Independence to today). Compiled 15 September 2026.*

**How to read this file.** Every factual claim carries a source URL and a confidence rating:
- **HIGH** — primary/official source (government gazette, ELSTAT, FAO/GFCM, ICCAT, EU law, peer-reviewed paper) read directly.
- **MEDIUM** — reputable secondary source (Wikipedia with citations, museum/municipal site, established NGO, quality press), or a primary source I could only read via a search-engine summary.
- **LOW** — travel/blog/tourism sites, single uncorroborated sources, or numbers whose ultimate origin I could not trace.

Where sources disagree, I say so. Anything I could not verify is listed at the end. Rigor over volume: several colourful claims that circulate online were left out because I could not source them.

## 1. The nineteenth century: fishing in the new Greek state

### 1.1 Context: what "Greece" meant for fishermen after 1821
The Aegean fishing world of the 19th century did not sit inside one state. The Kingdom of Greece (1830) initially held the Cyclades, the Sporades, the Saronic islands, Evia and the Peloponnese/central-Greece coast; the Dodecanese (Kalymnos, Symi, Halki) stayed Ottoman until 1912, then Italian until 1947; the North Aegean islands and Crete joined in 1912–13. This matters for the sponge story below, which is largely an *Ottoman-Dodecanesian* story until the 20th century. [general historical background; see the 1935 Kalymnos "Stone War" under Italian rule at https://www.kupi.com/en-ae/explore/greece/kalymnos/history, MEDIUM]

Shipbuilding was one of the foremost technical activities of the new state: "In the 19th century, shipbuilding was one of the foremost technical production activities of the new Greek state ... The most important shipbuilding center of that period was Syros, with Galaxidi, Hydra, Piraeus, Skiathos, Skopelos and smaller islands following. In the late 19th and early 20th century, there was a decline in the construction of large wooden vessels, mainly due to the emergence of steam-powered and iron ships." [Hellenic Ministry of Culture, Intangible Cultural Heritage inventory, "The craft of wooden shipbuilding", https://ayla.culture.gr/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Wooden_shipbuilding.pdf, HIGH]

### 1.2 The sponge-diving boom: Symi, Kalymnos, Halki

**Naked diving with the skandalopetra.** Before mechanised diving, sponge divers descended holding a flat weight-stone, the *skandalopetra*, and worked on a single breath. One diving-history site (drawing on Faith Warn's *Bitter Sea*, 2000, and Clift & Johnston's *The Sponge Divers*, 1955) describes "a 15 kilogram flat stone, known as the 'skandalopetra'" used to reach "about 30 meters for 3 to 5 minutes". [https://www.divingheritage.com/greecekern2.htm, MEDIUM] A Symi site adds that "In 1840, the first diver from Symi Michael Karanikis dived at the bottom of the sea, holding a flat stone weighing about 12–15 kilograms" and that Symiots were "the first Greeks to work on sponge diving". [https://www.allaboutsymi.com/sponge-fishing-and-sponges/, LOW — local tradition, no primary citation]

**The skafandro (standard diving dress) arrives, 1863–65.** The hard-hat diving suit — *skafandro* in Greek — was brought to Symi by the Symiot seaman Fotis Mastoridis, and his wife Eugenia famously demonstrated it by diving in Symi harbour. Sources agree on the people but disagree on the year and on where Mastoridis learned the technology:
- 1863, brought "from the Indies, where he had been working with the British on harbor works as a diving engineer"; Eugenia Mastoridou ("Kyra Fotena") dived in 1863; use spread to the other Dodecanese in 1864 and to the Saronic Gulf islands from 1866; "From 1863 to 1896, the number of diving suits across the Mediterranean reached approximately 440, while the boats of naked divers steadily decreased." [GreekReporter, 5 Jan 2026, https://greekreporter.com/2026/01/05/symi-tribute-first-female-diver-greece/, MEDIUM; corroborated by https://www.allaboutsymi.com/sponge-fishing-and-sponges/, LOW]
- "As from 1865 ... A merchant from the island of Symi brought equipment, probably Siebe Gorman, to the island", allowing work at "depths of about 70 meters". [https://www.divingheritage.com/greecekern2.htm, MEDIUM]
- "1865–70", with Mastoridis having seen the machine aboard an English vessel at the Cape of Good Hope; Eugenia, pregnant, descended to "15 metres" at Nimporios and brought up a stone as proof; early machines were rated to about 45 m, and a later French machine pumped air to 80 m. [https://www.greecewithin.com/articles/483-symi-island-the-history-of-diving-machines, LOW]
→ Safe statement: **the skafandro reached Symi in 1863–65 via Fotis Mastoridis and spread through the Dodecanese within a couple of years.** [MEDIUM]

**Fleet scale.** "By 1868, the sponge diving fleet consisted of '300 ships with divers (6 to 15 divers on each ship)', plus '70 ships that harvested sponges with harpoons' and '70 trawlers'." [https://www.divingheritage.com/greecekern2.htm, MEDIUM] Symi is said to have had "the world's largest sponge-diving fleet", dominated by merchant houses such as the Petrides brothers with offices across Europe and America. [https://www.allaboutsymi.com/sponge-fishing-and-sponges/, LOW] Kalymnos at its peak is variously given as "up to 350 ships with at least twelve divers on board". [https://www.kalymnos-isl.gr/en/about-kalymnos/sponge-diving and travel sites, LOW]

**The price: decompression sickness ("the bends").** The suit let men work far deeper and far longer, years before Paul Bert (1878) linked the disease to gas bubbles or Haldane published decompression tables (1908). The figures that circulate:
- "Between 1866 and 1895, on the island of Kalymnos alone, 800 young men died of the bends and 200 more were paralyzed." [Greeka, https://www.greeka.com/dodecanese/kalymnos/history/sponge-diving-tradition/; DAN Alert Diver, https://dan.org/alert-diver/article/the-story-of-sponge-divers/, MEDIUM-LOW — widely repeated, ultimate source not found]
- "Between 1886 and 1910 about 10.000 divers died and 20.000 got permanently disabled ... In the first years the standard suit was used, about half of the divers got paralised or died." [https://www.divingheritage.com/greecekern2.htm, citing Warn 2000, MEDIUM-LOW — these are Mediterranean-wide totals, and I could not trace them to a primary count]
- "Every household on the island could count at least one family member who had either died or been paralyzed during the sponge diving season." [Greeka, MEDIUM]

**Protest and the Ottoman ban of 1882.** "The women asked the Turkish sultan to forbid the standard diving suit, which he did in 1882." Profits collapsed with the return to naked diving, and "About 4 years later the suit was back", with the accidents. [https://www.divingheritage.com/greecekern2.htm, MEDIUM; same story at https://www.amusingplanet.com/2016/11/the-sponge-divers-of-greece.html and Greeka, MEDIUM] A 2024 peer-reviewed article reads a Symi protest poem against "the machine" from roughly 120 years earlier — i.e. c. 1904 — showing that opposition persisted into the 20th century. [Tandfonline, "Hit by the Machine: Reading a Local Protest Poem on the Island of Symi, 120 Years Later", https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1013929X.2024.2325769, MEDIUM for the article's existence; I could not read the full text]

**On the phrase "Sponge War".** I found no Greek/Dodecanesian event called the "Sponge War". Two things that *are* documented: (a) the 1882 ban above, and (b) the "Sponge Wars" of Tarpon Springs, Florida (1914), a conflict between Greek suit-divers and the Key West "hookers" over diving vs. hooking — reported by The National Herald (https://www.thenationalherald.com/the-sponge-wars-of-tarpon-springs-in-1914/, LOW — page could not be opened). The 1935 Kalymnos *Petropolemos* ("Stone War") was a revolt against the Italian administration over church/religious autonomy, not sponges. [https://www.kupi.com/en-ae/explore/greece/kalymnos/history, MEDIUM]

**A note on dynamite on Kalymnos.** A 2024 Marine Pollution Bulletin case study states that blast fishing had been carried out in the Aegean since at least the late 19th century and that "during the 1870s on Kalymnos Island banning dynamite fishing preoccupied the eldership of the island." [https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0025326X24003916, MEDIUM — seen via search summary; the article itself is paywalled]

**Emigration to Tarpon Springs, Florida (1905).** John Michael Cocoris (born Leonidio, Peloponnese) came to New York in 1895 for the sponge trade and worked with John K. Cheyney at Tarpon Springs from 1896. "In 1905, John Cocoris introduced mechanized diving techniques and began recruiting experienced divers from the Greek Dodecanese Islands (particularly Kalymnos and Symi)." [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cocoris, MEDIUM] The first suited dive in the Gulf was "June 18, 1905" [Hellas Bakery/local history page, https://hellasbakery.com/sponge-docks-history/, LOW]; "By 1905, over 500 sponge divers were at work using around 50 boats", the Sponge Exchange opened in 1907, and "by 1920 there were more than 1,500 Greeks living in Tarpon Springs, and more than 200 boats". [St Pete Catalyst, https://stpetecatalyst.com/vintage-pinellas-the-tarpon-springs-sponge-industry/, MEDIUM] The diving-history site agrees: "Around 1905 a group of about 500 divers from the Dodekanesos settled in Tarpon Springs". [https://www.divingheritage.com/greecekern2.htm, MEDIUM]

**Twentieth-century coda.** Italy, ruling the Dodecanese, is said to have banned sponge diving during WWI, hastening Symi's depopulation. [https://www.allaboutsymi.com/sponge-fishing-and-sponges/, LOW] A bacterial sponge disease swept the Mediterranean in 1986–90: "Disease outbreaks affecting bath sponges ... in the Aegean occurred from 1986 on"; at Marsala Spongia officinalis "disappeared ... in 1987, and reappeared only 5 years later". [PLOS One 2011, https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0020211, HIGH for the Mediterranean event; Aegean 1986 date MEDIUM] "In 1986 nearly all the sponges in the Aegean sea turned out to be infected", and by 2001 "from 400 ships, about 10 to 15 are left." [https://www.divingheritage.com/greecekern2.htm, MEDIUM] The Kalymnos municipality confirms that the disease forced the island to shift to other fishing, with "sponge boats replaced by vessels for tuna and swordfish". [https://www.kalymnos-isl.gr/en/about-kalymnos/sponge-diving, MEDIUM]

### 1.3 The caique (kaïki) and its boatyards

The Ministry of Culture's ICH inventory (the authoritative Greek statement on the craft) lists the surviving hull types: "the trechantiri, the gatzao, the botis, the tserniki, the perama, the trata (rowing boat), the varkalas, the Hydriot varkalas, the gaita of Chania, the karavoskaro and the 'liberty'. Some of these types, such as the gatzao, the tserniki, the perama and the trata, are extremely rare nowadays and are not constructed anymore." It notes purpose-built types: "the skafi from Symi, a sponge-fishing vessel that was primarily constructed in Symi" and the sponge/merchant trechantiri "named achtarmas ... primarily constructed in Kalymnos, Hydra, Piraeus and Symi." The key 18th–19th-century innovation was the lofting floor (*sala*), which "made it possible for shipwrights to construct even larger vessels with better symmetry". [https://ayla.culture.gr/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Wooden_shipbuilding.pdf, HIGH]

- **Symi.** Symi shipyards built "botis, yialadikes and achtarmades (sponge-fishing vessels), papadies, and trehantiria". [Museum-sector summary via https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/erasmuscliche-greek-traditional-wooden-shipbuilding/251075714 and the ICH file above, MEDIUM]
- **Skiathos.** "In 1833, the island demogerons permitted logging of thick forests, particularly the Aradia area above Kechria ... and intensive construction of sailing boats begun. Chief craftsmen were Konstantinos Floumis, Georgios Giannoulis and Dimitrios Alexandrou from Skopelos and the Skiathian Stamatis Stamatas", illiterate master builders praised by a British naval captain, James Kennedy, in a report to the Admiralty. [Municipality of Skiathos, "Maritime History", https://skiathos.gr/index.php/en/component/sppagebuilder/?view=page&id=288, MEDIUM] The island's Museum of Naval and Cultural Tradition documents the trade. [https://www.greekgastronomyguide.gr/en/item/mouseio-naftikis-politistikis-paradosis-skiathos/, LOW]
- **Skopelos.** The Bountalas family of shipwrights spans seven generations from the early 1800s, when Christodoulos Bountalas began building on the island; "the rise of tourism in the 1970s killed the market for giant working sailboats". [https://johnhendersontravel.com/greek-shipbuilder/ (2023), LOW-MEDIUM]
- **Perama (Piraeus).** Perama is the main boatyard district of Piraeus; experienced Samos builders historically moved there for work. [AP, "Greek traditional wooden boat builders a dwindling craft", 20 July 2021, https://www.dailyherald.com/20210720/news/greek-traditional-wooden-boat-builders-a-dwindling-craft/, MEDIUM]
- **Samos.** "Samos caiques are famed both for their workmanship and their raw material: timber from a pine species whose high resin content makes it durable and more resistant to woodworm." [AP 2021, above, MEDIUM] The Museum of Aegean Boatbuilding and Maritime Crafts at Heraio (≈300 exhibits, 200+ oral testimonies) opened and a Wooden Boatbuilding School — Greece's first — began in 2024–25 with €210,000 from the Ministry of Culture. [Greek News Agenda, https://www.greeknewsagenda.gr/wooden-boatbuilding-greece/, MEDIUM]
- **The scrapping tragedy.** The same article reports that "In the last 25 years alone, about 11,000 boats have been lost" to EU-subsidised fleet-reduction scrapping (Reg. 508/2014 and predecessors), which required the wooden hull to be destroyed. [Greek News Agenda, above, MEDIUM]
- **Trechantiri antiquity.** A forum claim that the earliest evidence for the trechantiri dates to mid-17th-century Hydra is unverified. [https://forum.woodenboat.com/forum/designs-plans/139831-trechantiri-type-boat-from-greece, LOW]

### 1.4 Gear: gri-gri, trata, pyrofani, paragadi, octopus pots, dynamite

**Gri-gri (purse seine with light boats).** The Greek purse-seine operation: a mother vessel tows a utility boat and several small lamp-boats; the lamp-boats scatter to concentrate anchovy/sardine under their lights, then the seiner encircles the school. A local-history account from Limenaria, Thasos, describes the gear as introduced there after 1922 by refugee fishermen from Asia Minor, and gives the etymology: "the name 'gri-gri' is attributed to the characteristic rattling of the rings" as the purse-line runs. [Politistikos Syllogos Kastrou (Thasos), https://politistikostokastro.gr/topos-mas/to-nafpigeio-tou-i-n-partsoli/, MEDIUM-LOW — a single local source; the origin of the word is not settled in what I could find] A 1950s Benitses (Corfu) memoir describes the same lamp-boat choreography. [https://atcorfu.com/benitses-photos-1950-celebrities/, LOW] Today anchovy and sardine "are the main targets of the purse-seine fleet". [Touloumis et al. 2026, Frontiers in Marine Science, https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2026.1766535/full, HIGH]

**Trata / vintzotrata (boat seine hauled with a winch).** The *trata* is both a rowing-boat hull type and the seine it hauls; the winch-hauled version is the *vintzotrata*. It is still counted by ELSTAT as a distinct gear ("beach seines": 119 vessels in 2023, 74 in 2024). [ELSTAT, Sea Fishery Survey 2024, https://www.statistics.gr/documents/20181/af9cd93c-496c-7c3a-02cc-caeaa06cbbba, HIGH] Its legal history is in §3.3.

**Pyrofani (lamp fishing with the kamaki).** Night fishing in shallow water (4–5 m) with a bow-mounted lamp and a harpoon (*kamaki*) for octopus, cuttlefish, squid and fish; two-person crews, one rowing, one striking. Lighting evolved from resinous pine torches (*dadí*) to acetylene (carbide) lamps mounted on the bow to today's battery LED lamps; for amateurs the lamp may not exceed 500 candela. [Kynigesia, https://www.kynigesia.gr/psarema/pyrofani-i-mageia-toy-psarematos/, LOW-MEDIUM; Mani folklore page "Φάκλα και Πυροφάνι", http://www.mani.org.gr/ithi/fakla/fakla.htm, LOW — site certificate expired, content via search summary] I could not find a Greek date for the adoption of carbide lamps; the nearest datum is Adriatic: "the first night fishing lamp powered by acetylene gas derived from calcium carbide ... was invented in Rijeka in 1898." [Jelsa Municipal Museum, https://muzejopcinejelsa.hr/en/2021/01/29/lights-of-the-past/, MEDIUM] So Greek adoption is plausibly early 20th century — but that is inference, not evidence.

**Paragadi (longline), nets and traps.** These "static" gears define the Greek fleet: at end-2022, 11,727 of 12,197 registered vessels (96.13%) carried "static gear only (mainly nets, longlines and traps)". [European Commission, Greek fleet capacity report, reference year 2022, https://oceans-and-fisheries.ec.europa.eu/document/download/6067c2d4-3f5e-4872-afc8-5d2d35e530f4_en?filename=2022-fleet-capacity-report-greece_en.pdf, HIGH]

**Octopus pots.** Pots for *Octopus vulgaris* are a recognised Mediterranean small-scale gear "deployed in specific seasons and areas, including the Gulf of Cádiz, Thracian Sea (Greece), and Gulf of Gabès"; in Greece PVC pots have replaced clay. [PMC7370935, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7370935/ and figure at https://www.researchgate.net/figure/3-Octopus-pots-a-PVC-pots-used-in-Greece-b-three-sizes-of-clay-pots_fig2_228994321, MEDIUM] I found no dated history of clay-pot use in Greece specifically.

**Dynamite.** Documented in the Aegean since the 1870s (Kalymnos, above). It has been illegal under Legislative Decree 420/1970 (the Fisheries Code). [Aplotaria (Chios), https://www.aplotaria.gr/fishing-with-dynamite/, MEDIUM] It never stopped: a 2002 report described organised networks with speedboats operating "mainly in the Aegean" and a "war" between professional fishermen and the *bourlotieridés*. [Ta Nea, 9 Feb 2002, https://www.tanea.gr/2002/02/09/greece/symmories-psareyoyn-me-dynamites/, MEDIUM] Archipelagos Institute reports repeated blasts and injuries around Koufonisia (Cyclades). [https://archipelago.gr/fisherman-gets-seriously-injured-using-dynamite-while-fishing-in-koufonisia-islands/, MEDIUM] **The often-heard claim that post-WWI/WWII surplus explosives drove a dynamite-fishing boom is plausible but I found no source that documents it.**

## 2. 1922–23: the Asia Minor Catastrophe and the fishing coast

Roughly 1.2–1.5 million refugees reached Greece after 1922, raising the population by about a quarter. [Pulitzer Center, https://pulitzercenter.org/stories/greece-ghosts-refugees-past, MEDIUM] Specific, sourceable fishing legacies:

- **Nea Michaniona (Thermaic Gulf).** Founded in 1923 by refugees from Michaniona in the Kyzikos district on the Sea of Marmara; the settlement recreated a Marmara fishing town and today has one of northern Greece's main fish-auction markets (*ichthyoskala*). [Greek Wikipedia, https://el.wikipedia.org/wiki/Νέα_Μηχανιώνα, MEDIUM; Visit Central Macedonia, https://visit-centralmacedonia.gr/en/where-to-go/55/1-thessaloniki/971/nea-michaniona, LOW]
- **Nea Chili (Alexandroupoli).** Refugees from Chili (Şile, Black Sea coast) built their own boat type there: "the chiliotissa, a type of fishing boat that was constructed in Nea Chili ... by refugees from Chili in the Black Sea." [Ministry of Culture ICH file, https://ayla.culture.gr/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Wooden_shipbuilding.pdf, HIGH]
- **Limenaria (Thasos).** Refugee fishermen settling after 1922 are credited locally with bringing the gri-gri purse seine. [https://politistikostokastro.gr/topos-mas/to-nafpigeio-tou-i-n-partsoli/, MEDIUM-LOW]
- **Keratsini (Piraeus).** Keratsini housed a refugee quarantine station and refugee quarters (e.g., Evgeneia). [Hellenic Parliament Foundation teaching file, https://foundation.parliament.gr/VoulhFoundation/VoulhFoundationPortal/images/site_content/voulhFoundation/file/Ekpaideytika%20New/racism/0_4racism.pdf, MEDIUM] **I could not find a source specifically documenting refugee *fishermen* at Keratsini or a refugee origin for its fish market — treat that as unverified.**
- A general point often made — that refugees professionalised Greek fishing and lamp-fishing — is asserted in popular sources but I found no academic treatment; Moutopoulos & Stergiou's "The evolution of the Greek fisheries during the 1928–1939 period" (Acta Adriatica 52, 2011) is the relevant paper and is cited in https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9774308/ [MEDIUM for its existence; not read].

## 3. The twentieth century: engines, trawlers, statistics, law

### 3.1 Motorisation and the data
"From the 1920s onward, most working boats in Greece were fitted with diesel engines, and by the 1950s, few still operated under sail." [Naftotopos article on traditional Greek boats, https://www.naftotopos.gr/images/n_Articles/16001/TradlGreekBoats247-02.pdf, LOW-MEDIUM] Malkotsis of Piraeus became the leading Greek marine-engine maker of the 1920s–30s. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malkotsis, LOW]

Catch history (reconstructed, Touloumis, Dimarchopoulou, Koutrakis & Tsikliras 2026): a 1914 estimate put Greek catches at 30,000–35,000 t/yr; statistics were collected for 1928–1939; continuous official series begin in 1964 (≈50,000 t); reconstructed total removals **peaked in 1994 at roughly 220,000 t** and had fallen to roughly 110,000–120,000 t by 2022 — "recession rather than recovery". Before 2016 the official survey excluded small-scale boats under 20 HP ("around 10,000" vessels), a major undercount. [Frontiers in Marine Science 2026, https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2026.1766535/full, HIGH] Greece joined the EEC on 1 January 1981, bringing it under the Common Fisheries Policy. [same, HIGH]

### 3.2 The GFCM
FAO's General Fisheries Commission for the Mediterranean was established in 1949 and its Agreement entered into force in 1952; it can adopt binding conservation recommendations. [FAO GFCM, https://www.fao.org/gfcm/about/en/, HIGH]

### 3.3 Greek trawl law — the actual text
Trawling (*mihanotrata*) is governed by **Royal Decree 917 of 27.9/12.11.1966 (ΦΕΚ Α 248)**, still in force as amended. From the codified text published by the Hellenic Coast Guard [https://alieia.hcg.gr/NOMOTHESIA/BD_%20917-66_en.pdf, HIGH]:
- Art. 1 defines trawling as fishing "by nets towed on the bottom by motor vessels in motion".
- Art. 2: minimum mesh 14 mm knot-to-knot wet (a 1980 amendment, PD 1063/1980, allowed 13 mm for 1980–81).
- **Art. 3 (as replaced by Art. 134 of Law 4799/2021): "Fishing with trawlers is absolutely prohibited during the months of June, July, August and September of every year, in the internal waters of the Greek Territory, as well as in a zone of six (6) nautical miles from the baselines."** → The summer closure the fisherman knows as *"1 June–30 September"* is real and statutory.
- Art. 4: trawling "absolutely prohibited throughout the year at a distance of less than one (1) nautical mile from the mainland and island coasts"; Art. 5 sets 2 nm in listed gulfs (e.g., Thermaikos, from Cape Poseidi to the Pineios mouth); Art. 6 sets 3 nm in other listed areas.
The Coast Guard's summary page adds further closures of 24–31 December and 24–31 May, and restates the EU rule: trawl nets banned "within 3 nm of the coast" or, alternatively, within 1.5 nm regardless of depth, and in water "shallower than 50 m", plus over Posidonia and coral. [https://alieia.hcg.gr/prohibitions/mhx/mhx.php, MEDIUM-HIGH]

The EU layer: **Council Regulation (EC) 1967/2006 ("the Mediterranean Regulation"), Art. 13** prohibits towed gear within 3 nm of the coast or inside the 50 m isobath where that is closer, and trawl nets within 1.5 nm regardless. [EUR-Lex, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2006/1967, HIGH] Greece tried to soften this: Ministerial Decision 164198/2008 and a March 2011 decision allowed trawlers to 1 nm along "45% of the Greek coastline", until the Ministry withdrew the exemption in 2011 after Commission pressure. [Archipelagos Institute, https://archipelago.gr/recent-advances-towards-the-protection-of-the-greek-seas-from-destructive-trawling-methods/ and the peer-reviewed policy paper at https://archipelago.gr/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/fisheries-policies.pdf, MEDIUM-HIGH]

**The beach/boat seine (vintzotrata).** Greece harmonised with Reg. 1967/2006 in 2010, in principle banning the gear over Posidonia and inside the coastal buffer; a 2007 decision had allowed it 1 October–31 March until 31 May 2010. [agro24, https://www.agro24.gr/agrotika/arthra/i-alitheia-gia-alieytiko-ergaleio-vintzotrata; Ministerial Decision 162570/2007, https://www.e-nomothesia.gr/kat-naytilia-nausiploia/kat-alieia/ya-162570-2007.html, MEDIUM] The Council of State upheld a permitting decision in 2010 (ΣτΕ 1675/2010). [https://nomosphysis.org.gr/15081/, MEDIUM] **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/929** then granted Greece a three-year derogation (3 June 2017–2 June 2020) for **244 boat seines** targeting picarel and bogue, on condition the gear "cannot operate above the Posidonia oceanica seabed". [EUR-Lex, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2017/929/oj, HIGH] A ministerial decision extended the last season to 31 March 2020, with the minister acknowledging "intense scepticism" about the gear. [Boat & Fishing, https://www.boatfishing.gr/vintzotrata-paratasi-kai-oristiki-katargisi/, MEDIUM] Yet ELSTAT still records 74 "beach seines" in 2024 — the current legal basis for those is unclear to me. [HIGH for the count]

### 3.4 Mid-century fisheries development
I found little specific documentation of 1950s–70s "fisheries development" programmes (FAO/Marshall-plan era). The Frontiers 2026 paper characterises 1964–1989 as a period of "possible gradual expansion towards deeper fishing grounds" within the same three fleet segments. [HIGH] Treat any richer narrative as unverified.

### 4.1 Bluefin tuna: crash and recovery
ICCAT judged eastern bluefin overfished in 1996; a 15-year recovery plan followed in 2006 (minimum size raised from 10 kg to 30 kg, seasons restricted). TACs: about 29,500 t in 2007–09 → 13,500 t (2010) → 12,900 t (2011–13); "The total catches decreased from around 50,000 tonnes over the previous decade to an average of less than 12,000 tonnes for 2010 to 2014." A management plan replaced the recovery plan in 2018 (in force June 2019); TAC 36,000 t (2020–22), 40,570 t (2023–25). [European Parliament Legislative Train, https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-fisheries-pech/file-management-plan-for-atlantic-bluefin-tuna, HIGH] **ICCAT Rec. 25-04 sets the eastern Atlantic/Mediterranean TAC at 48,403 t for 2026–2028, of which the EU receives 25,164.62 t.** [ICCAT, https://iccat.int/Documents/Recs/compendiopdf-e/2025-04-e.pdf, HIGH] Greece is one of the EU purse-seine states in this fishery, though far smaller than Spain, France, Italy. [same Europarl page, MEDIUM]

### 4.2 Swordfish
Mediterranean swordfish are under an ICCAT recovery plan 2017–2031 (EU Reg. 2019/1154): closed season **1 January–31 March**, minimum size 100 cm / 11.4 kg; "the EU accounts for some 70% of the total catches", led by Greece, Spain and Italy. [EUR-Lex summary, https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=LEGISSUM:4547330, HIGH] The plan cut the quota to about 9,000 t, less than half mid-1980s peak catches. [Earth Journalism Network, https://earthjournalism.net/stories/swordfish-scarcity-in-the-central-mediterranean-intensifies, MEDIUM]

### 4.3 Lessepsian arrivals
- **Rabbitfish (Siganus luridus, S. rivulatus; Greek γερμανός / αγριόσαλπα).** Both entered the Mediterranean in 1956; **first Greek record 1964, Dodecanese** (Kavallakis 1968, *Halieia* 248). [ELNAIS/HCMR, https://elnais.hcmr.gr/siganus-luridus/, HIGH] By 2014–15 at Rhodes, 6 of 49 gill-net species were Lessepsian and *S. luridus* was the most frequent alien. [JMBA, https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-marine-biological-association-of-the-united-kingdom/article/abs/alien-and-native-fish-in-gill-nets-at-rhodes-eastern-mediterranean-20142015/1E5D6A1DE96EB57CF6EADACACB19597B, HIGH]
- **Lionfish (Pterois miles; Greek λεοντόψαρο).** First Mediterranean record Israel 1991. [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pterois_miles, HIGH] **First Greek/Aegean record: Rhodes, published 2015** (Corsini-Foka & Kondylatos, *Medit. Mar. Sci.* 16(3): 692–702). [https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283119934_First_record_of_the_lionfish_Pterois_miles_Bennett_1828_from_the_Aegean_Sea; HCMR ELNAIS, https://elnais.hcmr.gr/pterois-miles/, MEDIUM] **Discrepancy:** the ELNAIS species page lists "First Sighting / 2009" while citing the 2015 paper; I could not resolve whether 2009 is a typo or an unpublished earlier sighting. By 2019 it was off Crete. [GreekReporter, https://greekreporter.com/2019/07/31/venomous-lionfish-spotted-in-waters-off-crete/, MEDIUM]
- **How many aliens?** 242 marine non-indigenous species were recorded in Greek waters by September 2020, most in the Aegean. [ELNAIS/Zenetos et al., https://elnais.hcmr.gr/ and https://www.reabic.net/journals/mbi/2015/2/MBI_2015_Zenetos_etal.pdf, MEDIUM] "There is an increasing number of alien species in the catch during the last decade or so, some of which are retained and marketed but most are discarded." [Frontiers 2026, HIGH]

## 5. Aquaculture: Greece and the farmed bream/bass

**Beginnings (1981).** Kefalonia Fisheries was founded in 1981 by Marinos Yeroulanos; the first sea-bass juveniles were imported from Sicily in 1982 and its hatchery reached 1.2 million fry capacity by 1985; the company claims to be the first Mediterranean bass/bream farm in Europe. [Kefalonia Fisheries, https://www.kefish.gr/en/about/the-story-of-kefalonia-fisheries, MEDIUM — company history] FAO: "Starting in the early 1980s, the first hatcheries supplied the first fingerlings for on-growing in cage farms." [FAO, https://www.fao.org/family-farming/detail/en/c/286479/, MEDIUM — seen via search summary]

**Scale today.** The Hellenic Aquaculture Producers Organisation (HAPO/"Fish from Greece") 2024 annual report: bream + bass production **114,500 t in 2024 (−5.5%)**, sales **€721 m (+3.5%)**, exports **94,132 t**. [https://fishfromgreece.com/en/annual-report-2024/, MEDIUM — figures via search summary; the PDF blocked automated download] A 2024 review counts **347 sea-cage farms** in Greece producing ~100,000 t (2020), with "Half of Greece's fish farms ... within 170 metres of the shoreline" and clustering on the eastern (Athens-side) coast. [van Pelt et al. 2024, Frontiers in Aquaculture, https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/aquaculture/articles/10.3389/faquc.2024.1383280/full, HIGH] FEAP places Greece among the countries producing "more than 100,000 tons annually" and FAO names Türkiye and Greece as the biggest bass/bream exporters. [FEAP, https://feap.info/data-home/data/, MEDIUM; FAO, https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/70e0420e-a432-4e10-beb8-1f5ff8ee07d1/content, MEDIUM] **"Europe's largest producer of farmed bream and bass"** is therefore correct for the EU (only non-EU Türkiye produces more). [MEDIUM]

**Where.** Farms concentrate in western Greece (Etoloakarnania/Echinades, Amvrakikos approaches), the Corinthian and Saronic/Evian gulfs, Argolida, Fokida and Kefalonia; the Frontiers review's "clustered within a few hundred kilometres of Athens" is the sourced version of this. [HIGH for the clustering statement; the regional list is my summary — LOW]

## 6. Conservation and the state of the sea

### 6.1 The National Marine Park of Alonnisos–Northern Sporades (NMPANS)
Founded by Presidential Decree on **16 May 1992**, "the first of its kind in the country"; about **2,260 km²**, "currently the largest marine protected area in Europe"; covers Alonnisos plus Peristera, Kyra Panagia, Gioura, Psathoura, Piperi and Skantzoura and 22 islets. Zone A (1,587 km²) includes Piperi with a 3 nm exclusion; Zone B (678 km²) allows amateur fishing under the Fisheries Code. MOm set up a permanent field team in 1990 and the Mediterranean's first seal rehabilitation centre on Alonnisos in 1991. [Wikipedia (citing Greek government and MOm), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alonnisos_Marine_Park, MEDIUM-HIGH; Greeka, https://www.greeka.com/sporades/alonissos/sightseeing/alonissos-marine-park/, MEDIUM] *Map note:* Kyra Panagia's older name Pelagos/Pelagonisi (cf. "Pelagnisi" on the 1683 map) — I did not find a source in this session confirming the identification; treat as plausible but unverified. [LOW]

### 6.2 The Mediterranean monk seal (Monachus monachus; Greek φώκια)
IUCN status: Critically Endangered (2008, when "350–450 individuals [were] estimated globally") → **Endangered (2015)** → **Vulnerable (2023)**; "The current global population is estimated at around 815–997 individuals of all age classes". [IUCN Centre for Mediterranean Cooperation, *Mediterranean monk seal: monitoring and research techniques* (2025), https://portals.iucn.org/library/sites/library/files/documents/2025-006-En.pdf, HIGH] It is **not** Least Concern. The eastern Mediterranean subpopulation is "no larger than 450 adult animals", mostly in Greece and Türkiye. [US Marine Mammal Commission, https://www.mmc.gov/priority-topics/species-of-concern/mediterranean-monk-seal/, MEDIUM] Older figures give "250–300 animals in Greece, with the largest concentration of animals in Gyaros island". [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_monk_seal, MEDIUM] A tourism blog's "350–450 in Greek waters" is uncorroborated. [LOW]

Fisherman–seal conflict is the core threat: deliberate killing is "the most frequently diagnosed cause of monk seal deaths in Greece"; one-third of deaths in the early 1990s and nearly 20% in 2011–12 were deliberate ("gunshots likely inflicted by fishers"); fishers reported seal damage to gear on 21% of trips. [MMC, above, MEDIUM] The emblematic case: **Kostis**, orphaned in 2018 (washed to Folegandros), reared at MOm's Alonnisos centre and released, was found speared "at close range" near Alonnisos in July 2021; MOm offered an €18,000 reward. [Washington Post, https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2021/07/27/kostis-seal-killed-greece-alonissos/; Guardian via PressReader, https://www.pressreader.com/usa/the-guardian-usa/20210728/281655373108144, HIGH]

**Gyaros** — the Cycladic island whose seal colony is among the largest anywhere — received permanent MPA status by Presidential Decree in April 2026, after a WWF-led effort begun in 2013. [Greek City Times, https://greekcitytimes.com/2026/04/25/gyaros-marine-protected-area-monk-seals-greece/, MEDIUM]

### 6.3 Posidonia meadows
Seagrass occurs along ~70% of the Greek coastline, covering **>2,749 km²**, with *Posidonia oceanica* dominant to 25–30 m. [Seagrass meadows in the Greek Seas (HCMR data, SEANOE), https://www.seanoe.org/data/00765/87740/ and https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362013636, MEDIUM-HIGH] Mediterranean-wide, Sentinel-2 mapping found 19,020 km² of Posidonia to 25 m. [Frontiers in Marine Science 2022, https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.871799/full, HIGH] Trawling and boat-seining over Posidonia are banned under Reg. 1967/2006. [HIGH]

### 6.4 The 2024 trawling ban and the 30 % target
At the **Our Ocean Conference, Athens, 16–17 April 2024**, Greece committed to "ban bottom trawling in all marine protected areas by 2030, starting from marine national parks by 2026", calling it "the single most effective" measure; the commitment document notes "fishing with trawlers (247 boats according to the official records)" and budgets "up to 30 million €" from 2026 for compensation/enforcement; Greek MPAs then covered 22,796 km², and Law 5037/2023 requires MPAs to reach 30% of territorial waters by 2030. [Greek government commitments, https://www.ourocean2024.gov.gr/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/A5_OOC-9_PROPOSED-COMMITMENTS_12.4.pdf, HIGH] Greece was reported as "the first country in Europe" to do so. [Euronews, 16 Apr 2024, https://www.euronews.com/green/2024/04/16/greece-to-become-first-in-europe-to-ban-bottom-trawling-in-all-marine-protected-areas, HIGH; National Geographic Society, https://news.nationalgeographic.org/first-in-europe-greece-bans-bottom-trawling/, MEDIUM] Trawler owners objected. [Maritime Executive, https://maritime-executive.com/editorials/greek-ban-on-bottom-trawling-stirs-controversy, MEDIUM]

**Two new national marine parks (2025).** Announced at the UN Ocean Conference in Nice (9 June 2025) and given boundaries on 21 July 2025: the **Ionian National Marine Park (~18,000 km²)** and the **South Aegean National Marine Park 1 – Southern Cyclades (~9,500 km²)**, ~27,500 km² together, incorporating 42 Natura 2000 sites (18 in the Southern Cyclades), with bottom trawling "entirely prohibited" and surveillance by drones, radar and satellite. [Euronews, https://www.euronews.com/2025/07/21/greece-is-creating-two-new-national-marine-parks-to-meet-2030-ocean-protection-targets-ear, MEDIUM-HIGH; PM's office, https://www.primeminister.gr/en/2025/07/21/36697, HIGH (page blocked to fetch; content via search)] The Special Environmental Studies were approved in December 2025, with Presidential Decrees to follow through the Council of State. [ProtoThema, https://en.protothema.gr/2025/12/29/the-plan-for-marine-parks-moves-forward-environmental-studies-approved-presidential-decrees-to-follow/, MEDIUM]

### 6.5 Overfishing — the regional picture
GFCM, *The State of Mediterranean and Black Sea Fisheries 2025* (released 28 Nov 2025): **52% of 120 assessed stocks remain overfished, down from 87% a decade earlier**; fishing pressure roughly halved 2013–2023 and assessed biomass rose 25%. [FAO/GFCM, https://www.fao.org/gfcm/news/detail/en/c/1754956/, HIGH]

### 6.6 The Greek fleet and catch — the numbers
- **EU fleet register, 31 Dec 2022:** 12,197 vessels, 62,642 GT, 359,977 kW; 11,727 (96.13%) static-gear boats, 97.55% of them under 12 m; 248 vessels with bottom otter trawls (157 trawl-only) and 313 with purse seines. Small-scale (<12 m) boats produced ~18% of tonnage; trawlers 24.7% (13,209 t). 266 boats were licensed for fishing tourism. [European Commission, Greek fleet capacity report 2022, https://oceans-and-fisheries.ec.europa.eu/document/download/6067c2d4-3f5e-4872-afc8-5d2d35e530f4_en?filename=2022-fleet-capacity-report-greece_en.pdf, HIGH]
- **ELSTAT survey of motor vessels (25 Sept 2025):** 11,416 vessels in 2024 (12,039 in 2023): 231 bottom trawls, 218 purse seines, 74 beach seines, 10,893 other inshore. **Catch 2024: 54,708.3 t worth €249.06 m**, −11.3% by volume vs 2023 (61,679 t). Inshore boats took 35% of the tonnage but **57% of the value** (€141.8 m). Series: 74,372 t (2016), 81,920 t (2019), 70,183 t (2020), 58,365 t (2021), 65,071 t (2022). [ELSTAT, https://www.statistics.gr/documents/20181/af9cd93c-496c-7c3a-02cc-caeaa06cbbba, HIGH]
- **2025:** 42,100.8 t worth €166.62 m; 11,399 vessels; purse seiners 51% of volume; Northern Aegean 28,452.9 t (67.6%), Southern Aegean 9,183 t, central Ionian 3,107 t. [Euronews citing ELSTAT, 4 Sept 2026, https://www.euronews.com/business/2026/09/04/greek-marine-fisheries-2025-production-worth-1666-million-anchovy-dominates, MEDIUM-HIGH]
- The "~14,000 vessels" figure sometimes quoted is out of date; current registers show 11,400–12,200. The fleet is ageing and shrinking. [SeafoodSource, https://www.seafoodsource.com/news/supply-trade/greek-fleet-declines-as-vessel-age-rises, MEDIUM]

**Why small-scale matters.** By number Greece has the EU's largest fleet, almost all of it under 12 m, and those boats earn more than half of the landed value on a third of the tonnage (ELSTAT 2024, above). "Coastal fishing, as an occupation, is particularly important for Greece." [EC fleet report 2022, HIGH]

### 6.7 New EU rules
Regulation (EU) 2023/2842 (revised Fisheries Control Regulation) entered into force 9 January 2024; most provisions apply from 10 January 2026, and electronic tracking and catch recording for the small-scale (<12 m) fleet from 10 January 2028. [European Commission, https://oceans-and-fisheries.ec.europa.eu/news/eu-fisheries-control-system-gets-major-revamp-2024-01-09_en and https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/2842/oj/eng, HIGH]

## 7. Culture

- **Kakavia (κακαβιά).** The fishermen's soup, named for the *kakavi*, "the tripod cooking pot used by ancient Ionian fishermen", made from the small, bony or unsaleable fish of the day's catch with olive oil and onion; commonly described as the ancestor of bouillabaisse. [Wikipedia (citing *The Essential Mediterranean Cookbook* 2005 and Chantiles 1992), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakavia_(soup), MEDIUM] The specific claim that it reached Marseille with Phocaean colonists c. 600 BC is a popular story I could not source beyond cookery sites. [LOW]
- **Psarotaverna.** The fish taverna needs no proof, but note the economic point above: the inshore boats that supply it earn 57% of Greek landed value.
- **Saint Nicholas, 6 December.** Patron of sailors and fishermen; his feast is "an official holiday for the Hellenic Navy, the Coast Guard, ships, and port authorities", with icon processions in seaside parishes attended by naval officers, sailors and fishermen; the coastline's small white chapels of Agios Nikolaos are thank-offerings for safe returns; boats are lit for Christmas (*karavaki*). [St Nicholas Center, https://www.stnicholascenter.org/around-the-world/customs/greece; Greeka, https://www.greeka.com/news/events/saint-nicholas-celebration/, MEDIUM]
- **Skiathos & Skopelos (map: Schiati/Scopoli).** Boatbuilding islands — see §1.3. [MEDIUM]
- **Skyros (Schiro).** Nothing fishing-specific was verifiable in this session. [unverified]
- **Petros the pelican, Mykonos.** A great white pelican nursed back to health by a local fisherman became the island mascot and died in December 1985, hit by a car; successors were named after him. Sources disagree on the year and rescuer: Wikipedia (citing the Chicago Tribune obituary) says **1958**; Greeka and others say **1954 or 1955** ("Captain Antonis Charitopoulos", "16 October 1955") and a fisherman "Thodoris". [Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petros_(pelican), MEDIUM; Greeka, https://www.greeka.com/cyclades/mykonos/sightseeing/petros-pelican/, LOW] Safe statement: mid-1950s, fisherman-rescued. [MEDIUM]
- **Santorini.** I found no source for a Santorini-specific "psarosoupa" tradition. [unverified]
- **Milos: the syrmata of Klima.** Fishermen's boat-houses "either fully or partially carved into the volcanic rocks ... just large enough to store a boat (typically about 6 meters long)"; name from *syrma* (wire/cable) used to haul boats in; found in 14 clusters (Klima, Mandrakia, Firopotamos, Fourkovouni, Areti); doors painted with leftover boat paint; 52 in Mandrakia now legally protected; most are now holiday lets. [Greek News Agenda, https://www.greeknewsagenda.gr/the-syrmata-of-milos-adaptive-architecture-and-cultural-heritage/, MEDIUM] Tourism sites date the present Klima settlement to the 1920s with upper floors added from the 1960s. [https://aegean-boat.gr/2026/03/02/klima-milos-the-colorful-seaside-village/, LOW]
- **Sponge-diving festivals.** Kalymnos still marks the fleet's departure/return with religious blessing and dance; details not verified this session. [LOW]

## 8. 2020s: notable stories from the Cyclades, Sporades and wider Aegean

1. **The killing of Kostis (July 2021, Alonnisos)** — §6.2. [HIGH]
2. **Marine heatwaves.** The Mediterranean's basin-average SST hit a record **28.5 °C on 16 August 2024**; the Aegean was ~28 °C and the Ionian 29.5 °C that day. [iMEdD Lab, https://lab.imedd.org/en/sea-temperatures-break-record/, MEDIUM-HIGH] A 2008–2024 satellite analysis finds an Aegean warming trend of **0.67 °C per decade** with heatwaves increasing in frequency and intensity. [Natural Hazards 2026, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11069-026-08026-8, MEDIUM] Greek researchers called 2024 the warmest for Greek seas in 40 years. [GreekReporter, https://greekreporter.com/2024/11/24/greek-seas-warmest-40-years/, MEDIUM] Warm-water species — round sardinella up 40% in 2024 (ELSTAT), parrotfish spreading north to the Saronic (iMEdD) — are the fisherman's-eye signal.
3. **The trawling ban and new parks (2024–25)** — §6.4. The Southern Cyclades park is the one that will touch Milos, Santorini and their neighbours. [MEDIUM-HIGH]
4. **Gyaros MPA decree (April 2026)** — §6.2. [MEDIUM]
5. **Blast fishing persists** around Koufonisia and elsewhere in the Cyclades; the 2024 case study documents catch and injuries from Aegean blast-fishing seizures. [Archipelagos; ScienceDirect, above, MEDIUM]
6. **Plastic and ghost nets.** Enaleia's "Mediterranean CleanUp", started after its founder saw Cycladic fishermen throwing net-caught litter back, reports **2,200 t of marine plastic collected since 2018 with 7,000+ fishers** involved; fishing nets were 16% of what reached recycling. [Enaleia, https://enaleia.com/, MEDIUM; Washington Post profile, https://www.washingtonpost.com/climate-solutions/interactive/2022/plastic-pollution-greece-enaleia-lefteris-arapakis/, MEDIUM]
7. **Lionfish everywhere.** From a single Rhodes record (2015) to Crete (2019) and now the Ionian; the Dodecanese/Cyclades small-scale fleet increasingly lands *Siganus* and lionfish. [§4.3, MEDIUM]
8. **Catch slump.** Official landings fell from 81,920 t (2019) to 42,101 t (2025). [ELSTAT/Euronews, HIGH/MEDIUM-HIGH]
9. **New control rules** phase in 2026–2028 for the small boats. [§6.7, HIGH]

## Things I could not verify (do not put these in the gift as fact)

1. **The exact year the skafandro reached Symi** — 1863 vs 1865 (and India vs Cape of Good Hope as Mastoridis's source). Both traditions are respectable; say "1863–65".
2. **The death tolls** (800 dead/200 paralysed on Kalymnos 1866–95; 10,000/20,000 Med-wide 1886–1910). Repeated everywhere, traceable only to popular histories (Warn 2000). Use with "reportedly".
3. **A Greek "Sponge War"** — no event by that name found; the only "Sponge Wars" are Tarpon Springs 1914.
4. **When acetylene lamps came to Greek pyrofani** — no Greek date found; Adriatic invention 1898.
5. **Dynamite fishing surging on WWI/WWII surplus explosives** — plausible, undocumented; what *is* documented is Kalymnos elders debating a ban in the 1870s and illegality since ND 420/1970.
6. **Refugee fishermen at Keratsini** and any refugee origin of its fish market; likewise a general academic claim that refugees "modernised" Greek fishing. Nea Michaniona, Nea Chili and Limenaria are the sourced cases.
7. **Origin and etymology of "gri-gri"** — only one local source (rattling rings); no linguistic authority found.
8. **Greek adoption date of the clay octopus pot.**
9. **1950s–70s "fisheries development" programmes** — no specific documentation found.
10. **Petros the pelican's arrival year** — 1954/1955/1958 all appear in print.
11. **Kyra Panagia = "Pelagnisi" on the 1683 map**; Skyros and Santorini fishing traditions — not checked against sources.
12. **Current legal basis of the 74 beach seines ELSTAT still counts (2024)** after the 2017/929 derogation expired in 2020.
13. **The ELNAIS "First Sighting 2009" for lionfish** vs the 2015 published first record.
14. **Greece's own bluefin quota share** within the EU's 25,164.62 t.
15. **Precise number of monk seals in Greece today** — figures range 250–450; the robust numbers are the global 815–997 and "≤450 adults" for the eastern Mediterranean.
