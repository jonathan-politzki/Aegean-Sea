## 2. FIX items (exact sentence, what the dossier says, suggested replacement)

### Stop 2 — Maroulas
**Sentence:** "On the east coast of Kythnos, the island the Venetian pilots called Fermenia after its hot springs, ..."
**Dossier 01 §3 (HIGH):** Mallet: "Les Pilotes *Italiens* luy ont donné le nom de Fermenia ou de Fermia, qui est le mot corrompu de Thermia, qui signifie des Bains d'eaux chaudes." Nowhere is "Venetian" stated.
**Suggested:** "...the island the Italian pilots called Fermenia, a corruption of Thermia, after its hot springs, ..."

**Sentence:** "Nearly a fifth of their stone tools were Melian obsidian, ..."
**Dossier 02 §3 (MEDIUM):** "c. 80 % local quartz and 16.8 % Melian obsidian". 16.8 % is closer to a sixth than a fifth.
**Suggested:** "About one stone tool in six was Melian obsidian, ..."

### Stop 3 — Cave of the Cyclops
**Sentence:** "Youra and Pelagnisi are now inside the Alonnisos marine park. Fishing here has a longer documented history than almost anywhere in Europe, and it is now the most protected water in Greece. That is the last stop on this walk."
**Dossiers:** Park membership is MEDIUM (02 §2, 05 §6.1). Neither "longer documented history than almost anywhere in Europe" nor "most protected water in Greece" appears in any dossier; 05 says only that the park was "the first of its kind in the country" and (per Wikipedia) "the largest marine protected area in Europe". And the park is stop 34, not the last stop — stop 35 (Thera, 2024–2030) is last.
**Suggested:** "Youra and Pelagnisi are now inside the Alonnisos marine park, the first in Greece. Fishing here has one of the longest documented histories in the Aegean, and the water is now among the most protected in the country. We come back to it near the end of this walk."

**Sentence:** "The oldest fish hooks in Greece come from Youra, ..."
**Dossier 02 §2 (HIGH):** Sampson 1998 calls the cave "the earliest site in the Aegean Sea" and reports "a variety of bone hooks"; 02 §0 says island fishing communities were "using bone fish-hooks" by the 9th millennium. No dossier states that these are the oldest hooks in Greece. Franchthi (older) yields no hooks in the dossier, so the inference is reasonable but unstated.
**Suggested:** "The oldest fish hooks yet found in the Aegean come from Youra, ..." (or keep, flagged as inference).

### Stop 9 — Minoan Crete
**Sentence:** "Then, late in the fifteenth century BC, the Minoan towns burned, and the record thins until Homer."
**Dossier 02 §7 (MEDIUM):** Marine Style is LM IB, c. 1500–1450 BC, "the last purely Minoan style before the LM IB destructions"; Mochlos LM IB c. 1500–1450 BC. The destructions are c. 1450 BC, i.e. mid-century, not late. "The record thins until Homer" is editorial; 02 §7 cites Privitera 2026 on LBA III lead sinkers, so the Mycenaean record is not blank.
**Suggested:** "Then, around 1450 BC, the Minoan towns burned, and the fishing record grows thinner for the next seven centuries."

**Sentence:** "Three bronze hooks from Kommos were first published as Minoan and later shown to be Iron Age and Roman."
**Dossier 02 §7 (HIGH):** findspots were "Hellenistic, Geometric/Archaic and 1st-century AD".
**Suggested:** "...and later shown to come from Geometric, Hellenistic and Roman levels."

**Sentence:** "...centuries before the Phoenicians made Tyre famous for it."
**Dossier:** only implied by the title of Stieglitz 1994, "The Minoan Origin of Tyrian Purple" (MEDIUM). Acceptable, but the Phoenician chronology itself is in no dossier. Keep, or cite Stieglitz's title directly.

### Stop 14 — Archestratus
**Sentence:** "And from another comic poet, Antiphanes: crawfish from Skyros."
**Dossier 03 §3.2 (HIGH):** Antiphanes, *Sheep-owner*, "crawfish from Scyros". Archestratus wrote a mock-epic gastronomic poem in hexameters (03 §3), not comedy, so "another" is wrong.
**Suggested:** "And from the comic poet Antiphanes: crawfish from Skyros."

**Sentence:** "Every other island on the plate, Milos, Paros, Naxos, Mykonos, Kea, Aegina, Santorini, Kythera, gets no fragment at all."
**Dossier 03 §3.1 (HIGH):** the no-fragment list includes those eight (plus Samothrace, Lemnos, Imbros, Skyros, Andros, Syros, Kythnos, Serifos, Sifnos, Ios, Anafi). But Lesbos — which `content.js` treats as a place on the plate (stop 15, stop 27) — has three Archestratus fragments (Eresos barley, Mytilene scallops, saupe at Mytilene). The Hellespont/Propontis also has fragments.
**Suggested:** "The Cycladic islands on the plate, Milos, Paros, Naxos, Mykonos, Kea, Santorini, and Aegina and Kythera besides, get no fragment at all." (Also note Skyros is in the no-fragment list even though Antiphanes covers it — fine as written since it is credited to Antiphanes.)

**Sentence:** "It is the oldest fishing guide to the sea on this plate."
**Dossier 03 §3:** it is a gastronomic tour ("where to buy the best fish"), not a fishing manual; Oppian is the fishing manual (04 §1.2).
**Suggested:** "It is the oldest guide to the fish of the sea on this plate."

### Stop 20 — Roman fish tanks
**Sentence:** "No Roman fish-salting factory has been found anywhere in the Aegean; the vat complexes are all in Spain, North Africa and the Black Sea, and the one regional garum town Pliny names is Clazomenae on the Ionian coast."
**Dossier 04 §1.1 (rated MEDIUM, and could-not-verify #1):** "No excavated Roman *cetaria* ... in the Aegean islands or Greece proper *turned up in this research*; the well-known vat complexes are in Iberia, North Africa, *Italy* and the Black Sea." Lytle 2018 (HIGH, abstract) concerns the Classical and Hellenistic Aegean, not the Roman period, so the source cited under this stop does not carry the Roman claim. Clazomenae (HIGH) is correct.
**Suggested:** "No Roman fish-salting works has yet been identified in the Aegean islands or mainland Greece; the great vat complexes are in Spain, Italy, North Africa and the Black Sea, and the one town in the region Pliny names for garum is Clazomenae, on the Ionian coast."

### Stop 21 — Oppian and Aelian
**Sentence:** "A generation later Aelian set that night fishing here, on Euboea, in the earliest description of what Greeks now call pyrofani."
**Dossier 04 §1.3 (HIGH):** NA 2.8 is "the earliest explicit *Aegean* description of what Greeks now call *pyrofani*". Oppian *Hal.* 4.635–646 (earlier) and Plato *Sophist* 220d "fire-hunting" (c. 360 BC, 03 §6) both precede it.
**Suggested:** "A generation later Aelian set that night fishing here, on Euboea, in the earliest description of pyrofani placed in Aegean waters."

**Quote cite:** "Oppian, Halieutica 4.640–646, trans. A. W. Mair"
**Dossier 03 §6 (HIGH):** the passage is given as *Hal.* 4.635–646.
**Suggested:** "Oppian, Halieutica 4.635–646, trans. A. W. Mair" (or verify the exact lines on LacusCurtius before printing).

### Stop 22 — Book of the Eparch
**Sentence:** "Of Leo's hundred and twenty other laws, a legal writer around 1080 noted that most were no longer used, but one still was: the law on the distances between fishing nets."
**Dossier 04 §2.5 (HIGH, Chitwood):** the c. 1080 treatise says "Lord Leon also composed 120 novels, but they are *not all* in use. Valid however is that which concerns [the distances between] fishing nets *and pieces of land*" — i.e. Novels 102–104 (nets) and 71 (land). "Most" and "one" both overstate; the fishing-net material is more than one novel, and land-distance law also survived.
**Suggested:** "Of Leo's hundred and twenty novels, a legal writer around 1080 noted that not all were still in use; the ones that were concerned the distances between fishing nets, and between pieces of land."

**Sentence:** "The fixed-net stations of the Bosporus, the epochai, were the most valuable fishing real estate in the empire, ..."
**Dossier 04 §2.5:** the term *epochai* and the distance rules are "MEDIUM by reputation, not verified here" (Dagron 1995 not read) and are could-not-verify #2. "Most valuable fishing real estate in the empire" appears in no dossier.
**Suggested:** "The fixed-net stations of the Bosporus, the epochai, were regulated property, and their Ottoman successors, the dalyans, were still being farmed out for tax in the eighteenth century."

**Sentence:** "The same court compiled the Geoponica for Constantine VII, ..."
**Dossier 04 §2.3 (MEDIUM):** compiled "for Constantine VII Porphyrogenitus" (Leo VI's son; reigned 913–959). "Same court" is loose across two reigns.
**Suggested:** "His son's court compiled the Geoponica for Constantine VII, ..."

### Stop 23 — Duchy of the Archipelago
**Sentence:** "When Mallet engraved the plate in 1683 only Tinos and Kythera were still Venetian; Tinos held until 1715."
**Dossier 01 §5 (HIGH):** Venetian survivals in 1683 were Tinos (to 1715) and Kythera (to 1797), *plus* the Cretan offshore forts Souda, Spinalonga and Gramvousa (lost 1715); the Ionian Islands lie off the map. Aegina was Ottoman in 1683 (Venetian again only 1687–1715).
**Suggested:** "When the plate was printed in 1683 only Tinos, Kythera and three fortress-islets off Crete were still Venetian; Tinos held until 1715, Kythera until 1797."

**Sentence:** "No Venetian fishing law or fish tithe for these islands turned up in the research."
**Dossier 04 §3.2:** the negative finding is specifically for *Crete* ("Venetian fishing ordinances or fish-tithes for Candia"), not the Cyclades. Also the phrase "turned up in the research" is a working note, not gift prose.
**Suggested:** "No Venetian fishing law or fish tithe for Crete or the islands has come to light."

**Sentence:** "...the trata, the beach seine and the boat that hauls it, is Venetian tratta, from the Latin for dragged."
**Dossier 04 §7 (MEDIUM, Wiktionary):** "τράτα is from Venetian *trata* < Italian *tratto* < Latin *tractus* 'dragged'". The Venetian form has one t.
**Suggested:** "...is Venetian trata, from Italian tratto and Latin tractus, dragged."

### Stop 28 — Hydra, Spetses
**Sentence:** "The boat that carried all of it, the trechandiri, the double-ender with the raised stem-head, is said by tradition to have been built first on Hydra in 1658 ..."
**Dossier 04 §7 (MEDIUM/LOW):** the Compton/Damianidis 1658 story is "a tradition – LOW as a dated fact", and "no period source names a *trechandiri*" before 1820. Nothing in any dossier says the Hydriot/Spetsiot merchant fleets (60–150 ships, 900 guns) were trechandiria; those were armed merchantmen. "The boat that carried all of it" is unsupported and probably wrong.
**Suggested:** "The fishing boat of these islands, the trechandiri, the double-ender with the raised stem-head, is said by tradition to have been built first on Hydra in 1658 ..."

**Sentence:** "Psara was third."
**Dossier 04 §4.3:** "Psara had the third-largest fleet after Hydra and Spetses, c. 45 ships ... [search snippet, LOW]".
**Suggested:** "Psara, by most accounts, was third." (or drop.)

**Sentence:** "...their fleet was crewed by Greeks doing their obligatory service..."
**Dossier 04 §4.3 (HIGH):** "an important percentage of sailors were Greeks". Not the whole crew.
**Suggested:** "...much of their fleet was crewed by Greeks doing their obligatory service..."

### Stop 29 — Diving suit
**Sentence:** "In 1905 about five hundred Dodecanese divers followed John Cocoris to Tarpon Springs, Florida, and built the largest Greek town in America."
**Dossier 05 §1.2 (MEDIUM):** "By 1905, over 500 sponge divers were at work using around 50 boats"; "by 1920 there were more than 1,500 Greeks living in Tarpon Springs". No dossier says "largest Greek town in America".
**Suggested:** "In 1905 about five hundred Dodecanese divers followed John Cocoris to Tarpon Springs, Florida, and by 1920 fifteen hundred Greeks lived there."

**Sentence:** "The suit spread through the Dodecanese within a year or two and to the Saronic islands, Aegina and Hydra among them, from 1866."
**Dossier 05 §1.2 (MEDIUM):** "use spread to the other Dodecanese in 1864 and to the Saronic Gulf islands from 1866". Aegina and Hydra are not named. Since this stop is pinned to Aegina on the plate, the link is weaker than the text implies.
**Suggested:** "The suit spread through the Dodecanese within a year or two and to the islands of the Saronic Gulf, Aegina's waters, from 1866."

### Stop 32 — Trawlers, law, numbers
**Sentence:** "Anchovy is the biggest catch, then hake, sardine and shrimp."
**Dossier 05 §4 (HIGH, ELSTAT 2024):** anchovy 11,921 t; sardine 5,569 t; hake 2,826 t; round sardinella 2,671 t; shrimp 2,663 t; octopus 2,045 t. 2025: anchovy 10,445 t (€19.55 m), sardine 4,542 t, shrimp 2,068 t, hake 1,949 t (€16.88 m). By tonnage sardine is second in both years; hake is second only by value.
**Suggested:** "Anchovy is the biggest catch by weight, then sardine; hake, the trawlers' fish, is second by value."

**Sentence:** "It is real and it is old: Royal Decree 917 of 1966, still in force, bans trawling absolutely in June, July, August and September, in internal waters and out to six nautical miles, and year-round within one mile of any coast."
**Dossier 05 §3.3 (HIGH):** Art. 3 in that wording is "as replaced by Art. 134 of Law 4799/2021"; the 1 nm year-round rule is Art. 4 of the original. So the June–September/6 nm formula is a 2021 amendment of a 1966 decree.
**Suggested:** "It is real and it is old: Royal Decree 917 of 1966, still in force and last amended in 2021, bans trawling absolutely from June to September in internal waters and out to six nautical miles, and year-round within one mile of any coast."

### Stop 33 — Syrmata
**Sentence:** "It is the same shoreline the obsidian left eleven thousand years earlier."
**Dossier 02 §1 (HIGH/MEDIUM):** earliest secure Melian obsidian at Franchthi is 11th millennium *BC*. From a 20th-century syrma that is roughly thirteen thousand years, not eleven.
**Suggested:** "It is the same shoreline the obsidian left thirteen thousand years earlier."

**Sentence:** "...with the family living above and the doors painted in whatever was left over from painting the hull."
**Dossier 05 §7 (MEDIUM):** Greek News Agenda gives the boat-store, wire etymology, 14 clusters, painted doors, 52 protected at Mandrakia, holiday lets. Upper floors are attributed only to a tourism site ("upper floors added from the 1960s", LOW). "Family living above" is unsupported.
**Suggested:** drop "with the family living above", or: "...each just big enough for a six-metre boat, often with a room above, and the doors painted..."

### Stop 34 — Marine park
**Sentence:** "The reward went unclaimed."
**Dossier 05 §6.2 (HIGH):** "MOm offered an €18,000 reward." Nothing on whether it was claimed.
**Suggested:** "MOm offered an eighteen-thousand-euro reward." (Verify outcome separately if you want to keep the original line.)

**Sentence:** "...the first in Greece and, at about 2,260 square kilometres, still the largest marine protected area in Europe."
**Dossier 05 §6.1 (MEDIUM-HIGH):** Wikipedia says "currently the largest marine protected area in Europe". But stop 35 itself reports the 2025 South Aegean park (~9,500 km²) and Ionian park (~18,000 km²); once decreed they dwarf it. The claim is also contestable on its own terms (it is a well-known Wikipedia line; other European MPAs are larger).
**Suggested:** "...the first in Greece and, at about 2,260 square kilometres, for three decades the largest marine protected area in Europe."

**Sentence:** "...the fisherman's rival since Homer put Proteus among his seals on Pharos."
**Dossiers:** no dossier mentions Proteus. Dossier 03 has Menelaus's men fishing on Pharos (Od. 4.368–369, HIGH) and "black seals" in the Hymn to Apollo (HIGH). The Proteus line is accurate general knowledge (Od. 4.400 ff.) but unsourced here.
**Suggested:** keep, but add the Odyssey 4 reference to the sources, or switch to the Hymn to Apollo's "black seals" which is already in stop 19.

### Stop 35 — A warmer sea
**Sentence:** "The sea on this plate has been fished for eleven thousand years, and this is the first decade anyone has tried to fish it less."
**Dossiers:** stop 32 (dossier 05 §3.3, HIGH) already describes the 1966 trawl closure, the 2006 EU Mediterranean Regulation, the 1992 park, ICCAT's 2006 bluefin recovery plan (05 §4.1), and dossier 05 §6.5 says Mediterranean fishing pressure "roughly halved 2013–2023" — so the effort to fish less predates this decade. "Eleven thousand years" also contradicts the dedication ("ten thousand years"); island fishing at Maroulas is c. 10,900 years old (02 §3), Franchthi fishing c. 15,000 (02 §1, Perlès "since at least the 13th millennium cal BC"), so either figure needs a consistent anchor.
**Suggested:** "The sea on this plate has been fished for at least eleven thousand years, and only in the last few decades has anyone tried, in earnest, to fish it less." And align the dedication ("ten thousand" → "eleven thousand", or vice versa).

## 5. Chronological ordering of the `stops` array

The array is meant to run earliest to latest. Problems, in array order:

1. **Stop 16 (bosporus, "600 BC – AD 20")** follows stop 15 ("345–343 BC"). Its start date is earlier than stops 11 (c. 520 BC), 13 (425–330 BC), 14 (c. 350 BC) and 15. Its end (Strabo, c. AD 20) is later than stops 17–19, and the paragraph also quotes Oppian (AD 177–180), outside the stated era. Either move it after stop 11 with era "600–300 BC" and leave Strabo/Oppian to the Roman stops, or relabel the era to the content actually used (e.g. "c. 350 BC – AD 20").
2. **Stop 17 (sifnos, "3rd century BC")** follows an era ending AD 20. Fine if stop 16 is relabelled or moved.
3. **Stop 18 (samothrace, "3rd century BC")** — the era is unsupported (Pancrates undated; see CHECK). If Pancrates is Imperial, this stop belongs after stop 21.
4. **Stop 12 (kythera, "antiquity")** sits between c. 520 BC and 425–330 BC but its witnesses are Archippus (c. 400 BC), Pliny (AD 77) and Pausanias (2nd c. AD). Thematic placement is defensible; a concrete era ("5th century BC – 2nd century AD") would be clearer.
5. **Stop 30 (sporades, "1833 onward")** follows stop 29 ("1863–1866"). Stop 30 is earlier; swap them (30 before 29). Section IV would then open in 1833.
6. **Stop 33 (milos, "20th century")** follows stop 32 ("1966–2026"). "20th century" begins before 1966; either give a concrete date range for the syrmata (the dossier's only date is a LOW "1920s" for Klima) or accept it as a coda.
7. **Stop 34 ("1992–2021")** follows "20th century" — fine if 33 is treated as a coda; otherwise 1992 sits inside 32's range.
8. Minor: stop 3 ("c. 8500–6800 BC") and stop 4 ("c. 7900–7500 BC") overlap stop 2 ("c. 8900–8400 BC") but start later, so the order holds.
9. Cross-reference errors caused by ordering: stop 3 says the marine park "is the last stop on this walk" (it is 34th of 35); stop 14's "the next stop but one" for Byzantium is correct.
